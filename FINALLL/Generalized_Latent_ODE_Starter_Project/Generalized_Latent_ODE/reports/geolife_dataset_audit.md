## 2. File format (verified, not assumed)

Every one of the 18,670 `.plt` files starts with the 6 documented header lines (`Geolife trajectory`, `WGS 84`, …). Every data line has
exactly 7 comma-separated fields: `latitude, longitude, 0, altitude (ft, -777 = invalid), days since 1899-12-30, date, time`.
Times are GMT (User Guide §4.1). The fractional-day field and the date/time strings agree to within 1 s on **every** line
(0 mismatches), so the date/time strings are used as the timestamp. Altitude is not used. The 69 `labels.txt` files (transport modes, GMT)
are used only to describe segments after the fact.

## 4. Cleaning and segmentation rules

The thresholds were fixed **before any model was run on GeoLife** and are not tuned on model accuracy. The audit supports them as follows:

| Rule | Value | Justification from the audit |
|---|---|---|
| Invalid coordinate | \|lat\| > 90, \|lon\| > 180, non-finite, or exactly (0, 0) | only 1 such record exists |
| Time order | keep a record only if its time is strictly later than the last kept record | removes the 698,900 duplicates; there are no backward jumps |
| Run break: recording gap | interval > 60 s | the 99th percentile interval is 20 s; 0.50% of steps exceed 60 s |
| Run break: implausible jump | step speed > 70 m/s (252 km/h) | the 99th percentile is ≈ 45 m/s (trains included). This also splits airplane tracks, which are outside the scope of ground movement |
| Segment | 200 consecutive clean records inside one run, non-overlapping (stride 200 from the run start) | the same number of points as the synthetic trajectories (200 on t ∈ [0, 10]) |
| Not stationary | some record ≥ 100 m from the segment's first record | otherwise the "trajectory" is GPS jitter around a parked receiver |
| Enough context / future | ≥ 60 records with t ≤ 7 and ≥ 10 with 7 < t ≤ 10 after time normalisation | 30 observations plus at least 30 withheld targets; a forecast set exists |

Result: 81,002 candidate segments, of which **75,100 are eligible** (5,875 stationary, 15 with too little context, 12 with too little future),
from 141 users and 12,266 files. Eligible segments last a median 6.6 min (5th–95th percentile 3.3–16.6 min). Their path length has a median of
2.6 km (0.5–12.6 km), and their mean speed a median of 6.5 m/s (1.1–31 m/s). The median record interval within a segment is 2 s.
The whole index, with the reason for every rejection, is `data/geolife/processed/segment_index.csv`.

Eligible segments are very unequally spread over users (median 192 per user, maximum 7,548), so the evaluation caps the number per user (§5).

## 6. Suitability for the 30-observation interpolation task

* **Density.** Sampling is dense and mostly regular (1, 2 or 5 s), so a 200-record segment has about 140 context records. Choosing 30 of them
  leaves ≈ 105 withheld recorded positions per segment as genuine interpolation targets. The 1,000 test segments contain 105,037 targets.
* **Scale.** Segments span roughly 100 m to tens of kilometres, while the synthetic training trajectories have radius ≈ 1 unit. Every GPS
  segment therefore needs a per-segment coordinate transform (see the main report).
* **Geometry.** Real movement is dominated by straight street sections and turns at intersections, not by sustained rotation. This is a large
  distribution shift from circles, spirals and ellipses, and it is the subject of the experiment.
* **Noise.** The withheld positions are themselves noisy GPS measurements, typically a few metres, so even a perfect interpolator would show
  errors of that order.
